Showing you heard · Lesson 2让对方知道你听到了 · 第 2 课

Notice feelings留意情绪

Listen for the feeling under the facts and name it gently, so the person feels understood, not just heard · ~10 min · reading and practice听出事实底下的情绪并温和地点出来，让对方感到被理解，而不只是被听到 · 约 10 分钟 · 阅读加练习

Step 1 · Facts and feelings第 1 步 · 事实与情绪

Learn先学

People often speak two things at once: what happened, and how it felt. “They changed the plan again.” is a fact. Frustration or worry may sit under it. Listen to the tone, the pace and the words they choose, and ask yourself what they might be feeling.

人说话常常同时有两层：发生了什么，以及感觉如何。“他们又改了计划。”是事实，下面也许是沮丧或担心。听听语气、语速和他们选的词，问问自己，他们可能是什么感受。

Step 2 · Name it gently第 2 步 · 温和地说出来

Learn先学

Offer a guess, not a diagnosis: “That sounds frustrating.” “I wonder if you are worried about the client.” A guess invites correction: “Not frustrated, more tired.” Be simple. A short “that sounds hard” often does more than a long speech.

给出猜测，而不是诊断：“听起来挺让人沮丧的。”“我在想你是不是担心客户。”猜测允许对方纠正：“不是沮丧，更多是累。”要简单。简短一句“听起来真不容易”，往往胜过一长串话。

Sort each one: good practice or weak practice?

给每一项分类：是好做法，还是薄弱的做法？

Step 3 · Do not tell them how to feel第 3 步 · 别指挥别人的感受

Learn先学

“Do not worry” and “it is not a big deal” sound caring, but they say the feeling is wrong. Accept it first. Feelings often ease once they are acknowledged. Only after that can you work on what to do about the problem.

“别担心”“这没什么大不了”听着体贴，却等于说这个感受是错的。先接纳它。感受一旦被承认，往往就缓和了。之后才谈得上怎么处理问题。

Fact: they changed the plan again. Feeling I guess: frustrated? worried? Say: That sounds frustrating. Is that right? Avoid: Don’t worry. / It’s no big deal.

Takeaways要点回顾

• Hear facts and feelings.
• Name it as a guess.
• Accept before fixing.

• 听事实，也听情绪。
• 用猜测的方式说出来。
• 先接纳，再解决。
