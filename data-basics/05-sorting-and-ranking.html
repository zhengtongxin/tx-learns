Sorting and ranking排序与排名

Put results in order and pull out the top few · ~10 min · reading and practice把结果排好顺序，并取出前几名 · 约 10 分钟 · 阅读加练习

Step 1 · Sort by a key第 1 步 · 按 key 排序

Learn先学

A pile of totals is hard to read; a ranked list is not. sorted(items, key=...) returns a new sorted list, where key says what to sort by. reverse=True flips the order so the biggest comes first. The original is untouched, which keeps the raw data safe.

一堆总数很难读，排好名的列表就容易多了。sorted(items, key=...) 返回一个新的已排序列表，key 说明按什么排。reverse=True 把顺序倒过来，最大的排第一。原数据不受影响，原始数据因此安全。

totals = {"Singapore": 220, "Penang": 80, "Ipoh": 150} ranked = sorted(totals.items(), key=lambda kv: kv[1], reverse=True) print(ranked[:2])

Step 2 · Top N and ties第 2 步 · 前 N 名与并列

Learn先学

Slice with [:3] for the top three. Two cautions. Ties: when two items have the same value, Python keeps their original order, which may look arbitrary, so decide how to break ties on purpose (by name, say). Small groups: “top city” of three rows says little; show the count next to every rank.

用 [:3] 切片取前三名。有两点要小心。并列：两项值相同时，Python 保持它们原来的顺序，这看起来可能很随意，所以要有意地决定怎么打破并列（比如按名字）。小样本：三行数据里的“第一名城市”说明不了什么；在每个名次旁边显示个数。

Takeaways要点回顾

• sorted(items, key=...) ranks without changing the original.
• reverse=True puts the biggest first.
• Break ties on purpose and show the count behind each rank.

• sorted(items, key=...) 排名而不改变原数据。
• reverse=True 把最大的放在前面。
• 有意识地打破并列，并显示每个名次背后的个数。
