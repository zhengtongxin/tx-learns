# Story for the AI FDE track

Setting: a three-storey shophouse on Tanjong Pagar's five-foot way in Singapore, home of Kaya Travel Cover, a small family-run travel insurer that has never used AI. The builder is posted there as a forward-deployed engineer: she works at their desks, not from a far-off office. (AI Agentic uses the Singapore kopitiam and AI Engineer uses the Block 12 void deck; do not mix them.)

Cast:
- Mei Ling: the forward-deployed engineer. Asks "what does your day look like?" before writing any code.
- Mr Goh: head of claims, thirty years on the job; trusts rubber stamps and checks every number twice.
- Aisha: senior claims handler; knows the odd cases and what a "fair" payout feels like (evaluation, human approval).
- Kumar: the one-person IT team; cares about keys, logins and what can break (security, deployment).
- Auntie Siew: front desk, remembers every returning customer by name (memory).

Props: the queue-number ticket machine, the red rubber stamp, the grey filing cabinet, the shophouse's old ceiling fans, the five-foot-way umbrella stand, the Friday afternoon rain.

Rule: each lesson ends with one short "Back at the shophouse" callout (.callout tip, EN and 中文) just before the takeaways, tying its idea to the shophouse.

## One thread: the claims desk pilot

Mei Ling builds one thing across the track: **the claims desk pilot** for Kaya Travel Cover. Constraint: Mr Goh signs off every payout rule; no automatic payout of a risky claim. Success: the queue clears faster at the same or better accuracy and cost.

- Arc by topic: (1) the job and the pilot's plan; (2) picking the model on real claims; (3) a harness that lets the agent work the queue; (4) measuring: costly mistakes, traces, a scoreboard; (5) knowledge and memory: the policy manual and Auntie Siew's customers; (6) pause for Aisha's approval, then parallel handlers with a cap; (7) a judge, an improver a human approves, guardrails and a gateway for Kumar; (8) hand it over as services and a copilot.
- Every lesson: a **Where we left off** box after the hero ("Last time the pilot could ___. Today it fails when ___. We fix it by ___.") and a **What changed** box replacing "Back at the shophouse" ("Now the pilot can ___. The next problem is ___.").
