Language models语言模型

See how a chat assistant writes by predicting the next piece of text, and why that sounds so fluent · ~10 min · reading and practice看聊天助手怎样靠预测下一小段文字来写作，以及为什么听起来这么流畅 · 约 10 分钟 · 阅读加练习

Step 1 · Predicting what comes next第 1 步 · 预测接下来是什么

Learn先学

A language model is trained on huge amounts of text to do one thing: given the text so far, predict what comes next. After “The cat sat on the”, a likely next word is “mat”. It picks a likely continuation, adds it, and repeats, one small piece at a time. Writing a whole answer is just this step done many times.

语言模型是用海量文字训练出来的，只做一件事：给定到目前为止的文字，预测接下来是什么。“猫坐在”之后，很可能的下一个词是“垫子”。它选一个很可能的续写，接上去，再重复，一次一小段。写出整篇回答，不过是把这一步重复很多遍。

Step 2 · Pieces of text and chance第 2 步 · 文字小块与概率

Learn先学

The small pieces are called tokens: often a short word or part of a word. For each step the model gives every possible next token a chance, and the chances add up to 100%. A real model weighs thousands of options; the example in Step 3 keeps three to stay short. It usually picks a likely one, but not always the top one, so the same question can get different answers.

这些小块叫词元，通常是一个短词或词的一部分。每一步，模型会给每个可能的下一个词元一个概率，所有概率加起来是 100%。真实模型要在成千上万个选项里权衡，下面的例子只留三个以保持简短。它通常选概率高的，但不总是选最高的，所以同一个问题可能得到不同的回答。

Step 3 · Fluent is not the same as true第 3 步 · 流畅不等于真实

Learn先学

The model is trained to continue text in a likely way, not to check facts. Because it has read so much, likely text is usually well-formed and often right. But a smooth sentence can still be wrong (“The capital of Australia is Sydney” reads just as smoothly as the true sentence), and nothing inside the model marks the difference. That is why a fluent answer still needs checking.

模型被训练成“用很可能的方式续写文字”，而不是核对事实。因为读得多，很可能的文字通常结构完整，也常常是对的。但一句通顺的话仍可能是错的（“澳大利亚的首都是悉尼”读起来和正确的句子一样流畅），模型内部并没有标记出这个区别。所以流畅的回答仍然需要检查。

Takeaways要点回顾

• A language model predicts a likely next piece of text, over and over.
• Each step gives chances that add up to 100%.
• The same question can get different answers.
• Fluent is not the same as true, so check what matters.

• 语言模型一遍又一遍地预测很可能的下一小段文字。
• 每一步给出的概率加起来是 100%。
• 同一个问题可能得到不同的回答。
• 流畅不等于真实，重要的内容要检查。
