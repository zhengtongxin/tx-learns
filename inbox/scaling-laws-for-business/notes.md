## Goal
Know what scaling laws mean for planning: bigger and more data tend to give better results with shrinking returns.

## Points
1. The observation: as models are trained with more computing power, more data and more parameters, their errors fall in a steady, predictable curve. Each step costs much more than the last for a smaller gain.
2. Business meaning one: models keep improving and getting cheaper per unit of ability, so something that is too weak or too dear today may work next year. Re-test every few months.
3. Meaning two: do not build elaborate workarounds for a weakness that a newer model may simply fix.
4. Meaning three: the best model is not always the right one; for easy tasks a smaller, cheaper model is enough. Match the size to the job.
5. Meaning four: a few companies can afford the frontier; you buy the benefit, not the race. Scaling is not magic: it does not remove hallucination or fix a bad process.

## What I got wrong (common slip-ups; tell them through Puan Rohani or Mr Tan)
- Assuming 'bigger' is always better for your task.
- Locking in a design around today's limits.
- Expecting scale alone to fix wrong answers.

## Try it yourself
Take one idea that failed or looked too dear. Write what would need to improve for it to work, and set a date to re-test.

## Takeaways
Models keep getting better with diminishing returns; re-test regularly, avoid heavy workarounds, and use the smallest model that does the job.
