## Goal
Say in business words what a large language model is good at, where it fails, and which risks the company must own.

## Points
1. Strengths: fast first drafts, summaries, translation, classifying messy text, answering questions over documents, turning rough intent into structure. It never gets tired and costs little per task.
2. Limits: it can state wrong things with confidence (hallucination); it does not know your private data unless you give it; it does not know yesterday's news; two runs can differ; long inputs get patchy.
3. Risks to own: wrong answers reaching a customer, private data leaving the company, copyright and licence questions, bias in decisions about people, a supplier raising prices or changing a model, and staff pasting secrets into public tools.
4. Controls that work: a human checks anything that matters, give the model your documents and ask it to quote them, keep a written rule about what data may go in, log what it did, and keep a plan B if the supplier changes.
5. Rule of thumb: the cost of a wrong answer decides how much checking you need.

## What I got wrong (common slip-ups; tell them through Puan Rohani or Mr Tan)
- Trusting a fluent answer because it sounds sure.
- Banning AI outright; staff then use it secretly on personal accounts.
- Counting only the cheap per-task price and forgetting the cost of checking.

## Try it yourself
Take one real task. Write its best case, worst case and the check you would add. Decide whether the worst case is survivable.

## Takeaways
LLMs are fast, cheap and fluent, and sometimes confidently wrong. Match the amount of checking to the cost of a mistake.
