## Goal
Write and defend a roadmap that mixes sure wins with experiments.

## Points
1. Three horizons. Now (0 to 3 months): proven improvements with clear returns. Next (3 to 12 months): bigger builds that depend on what you learn. Later: bets and research that may not work.
2. Give research its own protected slice of time and money, with different success rules: you judge experiments by what you learned, not by return.
3. Each item: owner, goal with a number, cost, review date, stop rule, and what it depends on (data, people, a vendor).
4. Defending it: lead with the business result, show the cost and the stop rule, show what is already working, and be honest about what is uncertain.
5. Review every quarter. A roadmap that never changes is a wish list.

## What I got wrong (common slip-ups; tell them through Mdm Rohani or Mr Tan)
- A roadmap that is only a list of tools.
- Research judged by the same return rules as production, so nobody dares experiment.
- No dependencies shown, so the plan slips when data is not ready.

## Try it yourself
Sketch a three-horizon roadmap with six items. Mark which are research. Write the two-sentence defence you would give the board.

## Takeaways
Mix sure wins with protected experiments, give each item an owner, a number and a stop rule, and review quarterly.
