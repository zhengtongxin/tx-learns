## Goal
Have better conversations with the technical people so decisions are made on real trade-offs.

## Points
1. Good questions: what is the simplest thing that could work? how will we know it is right? what is the worst it can do? what does it cost per month at today's use and at ten times? what would make us stop?
2. Ask for options in a fixed shape: option, cost, risk, time to learn, what we give up. Two or three options, with a recommendation.
3. Learn five words that save hours: latency (how long it takes), accuracy (how often right), hallucination, evaluation (a test set with known answers), and guardrail (a rule that stops bad output).
4. Ask for a small test on real examples before any big decision, and look at some results yourself.
5. Trust but verify: respect the team's judgement, and make them show numbers, not adjectives.

## What I got wrong (common slip-ups; tell them through Puan Rohani or Mr Tan)
- Nodding along when you do not understand; ask them to say it again with an example.
- Asking 'can AI do this?' instead of 'how good is it on our data?'.
- Letting the team choose both the question and the test.

## Try it yourself
Prepare five questions for the next tech meeting from the list above. Afterwards note which were answered with a number.

## Takeaways
Ask for two or three options with cost, risk and time, and ask to see results on real examples.
