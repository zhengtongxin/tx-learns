## Goal
Tell the kinds of AI supplier apart so you buy from the right one and know what you depend on.

## Points
1. Model makers sell the brains, usually through an API priced by usage. Cloud platforms rent computers and host many models, and add security and billing you already know. Chip and hardware makers sit under everything.
2. Application companies sell finished tools for one job (support desk, legal drafting, coding help); fast to start, less control. Integrators and consultancies build custom systems around your processes. Data and tooling vendors sell the plumbing: vector databases, monitoring, evaluation.
3. Offerings come in four shapes: a subscription seat, usage-based API, a managed platform, or custom build. Each has a different price curve and a different lock-in.
4. Questions for any vendor: who owns the data we send, where is it stored, can we leave and take our work, what happens when the underlying model changes, what is the price at ten times our volume?
5. Most companies end up with a mix: bought tools for common jobs, a platform for custom ones.

## What I got wrong (common slip-ups; tell them through Mdm Rohani or Mr Tan)
- Comparing a model maker with an application company as if they sold the same thing.
- Signing for a year before testing on your own data.
- Ignoring lock-in until the first price rise.

## Try it yourself
Pick one job you want help with. List one supplier of each type that could serve it and what you would depend on in each case.

## Takeaways
Know whether you are buying brains, a platform, a finished tool or a custom build, and what each ties you to.
