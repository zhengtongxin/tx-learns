## Goal
Show with numbers that AI paid off, in a way the finance head accepts.

## Points
1. Before you start, record the baseline: time per task, cost per task, error rate, volume. Without it you cannot prove anything.
2. Count all benefits that turn into money or measurable value: hours saved (only if used for something else or if overtime or hiring falls), faster cycles, fewer errors, extra sales. Count all costs: licences, usage, build, people, checking, training.
3. Simple formula: ROI = (benefit - cost) / cost. Also state the payback time in months.
4. Be honest: separate hard savings from soft ones, show a range, and use a comparison group or a before and after period where you can.
5. Report in a one-page scorecard on a fixed rhythm, and say what you will do next with the result. Stop what does not pay.

## What I got wrong (common slip-ups; tell them through Mdm Rohani or Mr Tan)
- Claiming saved hours that never became anything.
- Forgetting the checking time and training in the cost.
- No baseline, so the number is a guess.

## Try it yourself
For one use case, set the baseline numbers, estimate the monthly cost and benefit, and compute payback in months. Ask a finance colleague to challenge it.

## Takeaways
Measure the baseline first, count all costs, report a range and the payback, and stop what does not pay.
