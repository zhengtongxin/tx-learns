## Goal
Understand what a sponsor must actually decide, and what to leave to the team.

## Points
1. A sponsor owns the why, the limits and the answer to 'is it working?'. Not the code.
2. Five decisions only you can make: the goal in one sentence with a number; the budget and the date to review; what data may be used; what the system may never do on its own; who is accountable if it goes wrong.
3. Decisions to delegate: which model, which tools, how to build it, what to log. Ask for the reasoning in plain words and a recommendation, not a menu.
4. Say yes with conditions: 'yes for four weeks on one store, we review against this number'.
5. Show up: a sponsor who disappears leaves the team guessing; a short weekly look at the real numbers is enough.

## What I got wrong (common slip-ups; tell them through Puan Rohani or Mr Tan)
- Approving a vague goal like 'use AI to improve service'.
- Deciding technical details you do not understand, or deciding nothing at all.
- Never defining what counts as success, so the project cannot end.

## Try it yourself
Write the five sponsor decisions for one idea in five lines. If any line is 'we will see', that is the gap.

## Takeaways
A sponsor decides the goal, the limits and who is accountable, and checks progress against a number.
