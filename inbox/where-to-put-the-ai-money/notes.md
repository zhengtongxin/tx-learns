## Goal
Make strategic and investment choices: which AI bets, how big, and in what order.

## Points
1. Three kinds of bet. Cost bets: do the same work for less. Revenue bets: sell more or sell something new. Defence bets: do it because rivals will and standing still costs you.
2. Score each idea on value (dollars or hours), effort, risk and how soon you learn if it works. Prefer small, quick, reversible bets first; keep a few bigger ones that could change the business.
3. A simple split many leaders use: most of the budget on proven, boring improvements, some on promising bets, a little on wild experiments.
4. Build, buy or partner: buy what everyone needs, build what makes you different, partner when you lack people.
5. Fund in stages with a stop rule: money for a month, a number to hit, a person who can say stop.

## What I got wrong (common slip-ups; tell them through Mdm Rohani or Mr Tan)
- Funding a big flashy project because a rival announced one.
- Spreading money thinly across ten pilots nobody owns.
- No stop rule, so a failing bet lives on.

## Try it yourself
Write six ideas, score each from 1 to 5 on value, effort and risk, and pick one cost bet, one revenue bet and one to say no to.

## Takeaways
Fund small reversible bets first, in stages, with a stop rule and a named owner.
